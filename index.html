About Me

I am a Ph.D. student in Statistics and Data Science at Washington University in St. Louis, advised by Prof. Chao Cheng and co-advised by Prof. Xuming He.

My research interests lie in causal inference, semiparametric theory, and robust policy learning. I am particularly interested in how statistical methods can support reliable decisions for heterogeneous and interacting populations, especially when data are limited or modeling assumptions are uncertain.

Before joining WashU, I received an M.A. in Statistics from Columbia University and a B.S. in Financial Mathematics and Statistics, with a minor in German Studies, from the University of California, Santa Barbara.

Research Interests

  • Causal inference and semiparametric theory
  • Causal mediation and interference
  • Robust policy learning and decision-making
  • Sensitivity analysis and partial identification
  • Statistical machine learning and Bayesian computation

Research

Causal Mediation under Clustered Interference

2026–present

Graduate research · Washington University in St. Louis
Advisor: Chao Cheng

Developing identification and semiparametric estimation methods for direct, mediated, cross-mediated, and spillover effects under clustered interference and stochastic treatment policies. My current work also studies robustness to nuisance-model misspecification and flexible estimation with machine learning.
